\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

This research addresses the robustness of Persian automatic speech recognition (ASR) under real-world telephone and Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) conditions. A major barrier is the mismatch between training data and speech affected by environmental noise, bandwidth limitations, codec compression, and packet loss. Collecting labeled calls is also costly and involves privacy concerns. Pretrained speech models still require adaptation to the target language and conditions. Although previous studies have explored training with degraded data, speech enhancement, multi-view fusion, and unlabeled speech, many of these approaches remain insufficiently studied for Persian ASR under telecommunication conditions.

This thesis aims to develop a practical, reproducible approach without large-scale collection of labeled calls. Two complementary directions were investigated: a data-driven approach using simulated telecommunication conditions and additional Persian speech, and a model-driven approach assessing whether enhancement-fusion could extract complementary information from degraded and enhanced speech beyond multi-condition training alone. The main contribution is a common experimental framework for examining the effects of telecommunication degradations, architectural complexity, and training data volume separately, and assessing whether improvements transfer to real telephone and VoIP speech.

In the data-driven approach, Whisper Small was trained under multi-condition settings on a mixture of original speech and speech subjected to telecommunication degradations. IranSeda audiobooks were also segmented, pseudo-labeled, filtered, and added to the most comprehensive training configuration to examine the effect of more data without a direct match to the telephone channel. In the model-driven approach, a dual-stream system processed the original and enhanced representations simultaneously and combined them through a learnable mechanism. All main approaches were evaluated under a common protocol across Persian datasets covering general-domain speech and real telephone and VoIP calls.

Multi-condition training, without changing the ASR architecture, reduced the aggregate word error rate from $23.27\%$ to $17.83\%$, a relative reduction of $23.4\%$. This improvement transferred to real telecommunication speech, reducing the word error rate on the telephone and VoIP evaluation set from $35.63\%$ to $25.80\%$. Fusion achieved an aggregate word error rate of $17.64\%$ and character error rate of $7.85\%$, slightly outperforming the multi-condition baseline. Adding pseudo-labeled speech further reduced the aggregate word error rate from $17.83\%$ to $17.22\%$, with little change in performance on real calls. These findings identify multi-condition training as the most effective approach under telecommunication conditions, with modest further gains from fusion and benefits from additional data that depend on its match to the target conditions.

\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:
Persian automatic speech recognition, domain mismatch, telephone speech, Voice over Internet Protocol, multi-condition training, telecommunications data augmentation, pseudo-labeling, dual-stream fusion
