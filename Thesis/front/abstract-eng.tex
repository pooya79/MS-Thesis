\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

The mismatch between conventional training data and real telephone and Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) speech is a major obstacle to robust Persian automatic speech recognition. Pretrained speech models partly alleviate data scarcity, but they do not eliminate the need for adaptation to the target language and channel. At the same time, collecting transcribed Persian telephone calls is costly and raises privacy concerns. Prior work has largely studied training with degraded data, speech enhancement and fusion, and the use of unlabeled audio as separate approaches. Consequently, their interaction in Persian ASR and their value relative to a strong multi-condition baseline remain insufficiently understood.

This thesis aims to design and evaluate a practical, reproducible approach to robust Persian speech recognition without relying on the large-scale collection of labeled telephone calls. Two routes are pursued. The data-driven route targets robustness by bringing training conditions closer to the deployment channel and by increasing the amount of Persian speech available for training. The model-driven route examines whether an enhancement--fusion architecture can extract complementary information from noisy and enhanced views beyond what is already learned through multi-condition training. The principal innovation is to place these routes in a controlled sequence of comparisons that disentangles the roles of channel-relevant data, model complexity, and out-of-domain data, while testing whether robustness learned from synthetic degradations transfers to real telecommunications speech.

In the data-driven route, traceable telecommunications degradations are applied to Persian speech, and Whisper-small is trained under multiple conditions using both conventional and degraded speech. Long-form speech from Iran Seda is then segmented, pseudo-labeled, filtered, and added to the most complete training mixture to examine the effects of data scale and domain match. In the model-driven route, a dual-view system is developed to combine the original and enhanced representations through a learned mechanism. All routes are evaluated under a common protocol on several Persian datasets covering both general-domain speech and real telephone and VoIP calls.

The results show that multi-condition training substantially reduces overall recognition error without modifying the recognizer architecture, and that this improvement transfers to real telecommunications speech. The model-driven route performs better than conventional training but does not surpass the multi-condition baseline; its learned gate also relies almost exclusively on the original view. Adding pseudo-labeled speech improves overall performance and several general-domain conditions, but it does not increase robustness on real telephone calls. Small differences should be interpreted cautiously because the experiments do not include multiple training seeds or statistical significance testing.

Based on this evidence, multi-condition Whisper-small is proposed as the selected model. Without additional architectural complexity or transcribed telephone calls in training, it provides a clear improvement on real telecommunications speech. The central finding is that, for this problem, matching the training data to the target channel is more consequential than adding architectural components or merely increasing the amount of training data.

\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:
Persian automatic speech recognition, domain mismatch, telephone speech, Voice over IP, multi-condition training, telecommunications data augmentation, pseudo-labeling, dual-view fusion
