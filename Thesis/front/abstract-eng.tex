\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

This research addresses improving the robustness of Persian automatic speech recognition (ASR) under real-world telephone and Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) conditions. The mismatch between commonly used training data and speech encountered under such conditions is one of the main barriers to robust recognition: environmental noise, bandwidth limitations, codec compression, and packet loss can alter the acoustic cues of speech. At the same time, collecting and transcribing real Persian telephone conversations is costly and involves privacy concerns. Pretrained speech models can partially alleviate data scarcity, but large-scale pretraining does not eliminate the need for adaptation to the target language and operating conditions. Previous studies have investigated training with degraded data, speech enhancement, multi-view fusion, and the use of unlabeled speech as possible approaches to improving robustness. However, many of these approaches have not yet been studied extensively and systematically for Persian ASR, particularly under telephone and VoIP conditions.

The objective of this thesis is to design and evaluate a practical and reproducible approach for improving the robustness of Persian ASR without relying on the large-scale collection of labeled telephone conversations. Two complementary directions were investigated. The first was data-driven and aimed to improve robustness by simulating telephone and VoIP conditions in the training data and by increasing the amount of available Persian speech. The second was model-driven and examined whether an enhancement-fusion architecture could extract complementary information from the degraded and enhanced speech views beyond what could be achieved through multi-condition training alone. The main contribution of this work is the evaluation of these approaches within a common experimental framework, allowing the effects of training with telecommunication degradations, introducing a more complex architecture, and adding more training data to be examined separately. This framework also makes it possible to assess the extent to which improvements obtained under simulated conditions persist in real telephone and VoIP speech.

In the data-driven approach, a set of traceable telecommunication degradations was applied to Persian speech, and Whisper Small was trained under multi-condition settings using a mixture of original and degraded speech. As a complementary approach, IranSeda audiobooks were segmented, pseudo-labeled, and filtered before being added to the most comprehensive training configuration. This experiment was designed to determine the effect of increasing the amount of training data even when the additional speech did not directly match the telephone channel. In the model-driven approach, a dual-stream system was developed to process the original and enhanced speech representations simultaneously and combine them using a learnable mechanism. All main approaches were evaluated under a common protocol across several Persian datasets covering both general-domain speech and real telephone and VoIP calls.

The results show that multi-condition training, without any modification to the ASR architecture, reduced the aggregate word error rate from $23.27\%$ to $17.83\%$, corresponding to a relative error reduction of $23.4\%$. This improvement also transferred to real telecommunication speech, reducing the word error rate on the telephone and VoIP evaluation set from $35.63\%$ to $25.80\%$. The model-driven approach, with an aggregate word error rate of $18.44\%$, outperformed conventional training but did not surpass the multi-condition baseline. Adding pseudo-labeled speech further reduced the aggregate word error rate from $17.83\%$ to $17.22\%$ while maintaining performance close to the previous level on real telephone calls.

Based on this evidence, the multi-condition Whisper Small model is proposed as the preferred model for the target application. Without introducing additional architectural complexity or using transcribed telephone conversations during training, it achieved a clear improvement on real telecommunication speech. The main conclusion of this thesis is that, in this low-resource setting, matching the training data to the target channel is more important than directly adding architectural components or merely increasing the amount of training data.

\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:
Persian automatic speech recognition, domain mismatch, telephone speech, Voice over Internet Protocol, multi-condition training, telecommunications data augmentation, pseudo-labeling, dual-stream fusion
