\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

One of the main challenges in robust Persian automatic speech recognition is the mismatch between conventional training data and real speech from telephone calls and Voice over IP (VoIP) systems. This thesis investigates two reproducible, data-centric approaches to reducing this mismatch and addressing data scarcity: multi-condition training with telecommunications degradations and training-data expansion using pseudo-labeled long-form speech. For the first approach, aligned clean--degraded pairs are constructed from Persian speech, and environmental noise, gain variation, bandwidth restriction, real audio-codec round trips through FFmpeg, and packet loss are applied with recorded random seeds and metadata. Whisper-small is then fine-tuned first on ordinary data and long-form variants and again with the addition of degraded data.

To assess whether architectural complexity provides benefits beyond multi-condition training, a dual-view model is also designed. It processes the original and enhanced log-Mel representations with a shared Whisper encoder and combines them using bidirectional cross-attention and a time--feature gate. Its enhancement module is a residual U-Net with temporal modeling, and the complete system is prepared through three stages culminating in joint end-to-end training. For the second data-centric approach, 250 hours are selected from 3,777 Iran Seda audiobooks comprising approximately 6,300 hours of raw audio. The selected speech is segmented, pseudo-labeled using a Persian-adapted Whisper-medium model, conservatively corrected, and filtered. Accepted samples are added to the complete multi-condition Whisper-small training mixture without changing the architecture. A Persian Whisper-medium model is also evaluated in a separate experiment to examine the observed effect of increased model capacity.

The models are evaluated on five Persian datasets, including real telephone and VoIP speech from AGFarsdat. In the controlled Whisper-small comparison, multi-condition training reduces the aggregate word error rate from 23.27\% to 17.83\% and the character error rate from 10.84\% to 7.93\%. The fusion model, with a word error rate of 18.44\%, outperforms the ordinary model but not the multi-condition model, while its character error rate reaches 14.04\%. The mean gate weights for the enhanced and original views are 0.0039 and 0.9961, respectively, indicating that the model relies almost entirely on the original stream. Although fusion reduces the word error rate on AGFarsdat by 0.42 percentage points relative to the multi-condition model, this small difference cannot be considered a stable advantage without statistical testing.

Adding Iran Seda data brings the aggregate word and character error rates of multi-condition Whisper-small to 17.22\% and 7.70\%, respectively, but its effect is domain-dependent: performance improves on three general-domain datasets, whereas the word error rate on AGFarsdat increases from 25.80\% to 26.66\%. Whisper-medium achieves the best observed aggregate result, with a word error rate of 14.36\% and a character error rate of 6.68\%; however, because it has greater capacity, its difference from the smaller models is not a controlled, purely data-centric comparison. Overall, traceable telecommunications data augmentation and multi-condition training provide the clearest baseline for robustness to telecommunications conditions. The current fusion architecture yields no additional aggregate benefit, while pseudo-labeled long-form speech primarily improves general-domain performance rather than recognition of real telecommunications speech.

\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:
Persian automatic speech recognition, telephone speech, Voice over IP, Whisper, multi-condition training, pseudo-labeling, long-form speech, dual-view fusion
